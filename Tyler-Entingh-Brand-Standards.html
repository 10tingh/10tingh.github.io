How to use it
  • Espresso and Cocoa carry every paragraph. The safest defaults, at any size.
  • Burnt Clay clears full AA on white (5.2:1). It's the one accent cleared for body-weight text, not just headlines — moving off a cream ground earned this.
  • Ochre and Harvest Gold stay display-only. 24px+/bold, or as a fill with Espresso set on top.
  • Paperwhite is the ground, full stop. Manila and Sand step in for panels and the wave motif — never for the whole page.

Identity mark

Mark

Two ways to sign the work: a striped roundel for small spaces — favicon, social avatar, business card — and a set wordmark for anywhere the full name fits.

Roundel — 24px minimum
TE
Wordmark — headers & letterhead
Tyler Entingh
Clear space & minimum size
  • Keep clear space equal to the height of the "T" on all sides — nothing else crosses that margin.
  • Roundel drops below 24px on screen; below that, use initials alone, no ring.
  • Wordmark never gets condensed, stretched, or set in anything but Fraunces.
  • Use the roundel where space is tight and unattended (favicon, avatar); use the wordmark wherever the full name reads comfortably.

07
Do / don't

Usage rules

Do

  • Run one Fraunces headline per composition — let it be the loud thing.
  • Set every paragraph in Espresso or Cocoa on Paperwhite.
  • Use Burnt Clay as the single call-to-action / accent color per screen.
  • Let Paperwhite breathe — open white space is what keeps this feeling current, not dated.
  • Use the mono face for anything factual: dates, contact info, specs.

Don't

  • Set body copy in Harvest Gold — it won't clear contrast at any size.
  • Stack more than one motif (wave + diagonal stripes, etc.) in one composition.
  • Condense, stretch, or italicize Fraunces headlines for a tighter fit.
  • Tint the whole page cream or beige — Paperwhite is the ground; warmth comes from the accents.
  • Border every panel — let white space and the wave motif carry the rhythm instead.
