Who I am

About

I've spent 10+ years leading product design end to end, across SaaS, enterprise, and startup teams, and I still love the craft as much as day one. Lately, I've been deep in AI-powered products, for internal teams and enterprise clients alike, using modern AI-assisted development tools to take ideas from a napkin sketch to shipped software fast. I know how to turn user research and business goals into design that's genuinely accessible and high-impact, and I know how to lead a cross-functional team through the whole product lifecycle without losing momentum. Below are some skills I like to highlight for folks I work with:

01 / Strategic operator

Understands the business and technical side of digital products.

Pitches new business, keeps the team on track to deadlines, and does the design work firsthand.

Practical example: At both SeeSaw Labs and Slide UX, I've been heavily involved in the sales process for new client engagements, and I've owned projects from the PM seat: leading the team against milestones and deadlines, keeping clients closely informed on progress, and spotting opportunities to grow further into an account for real business impact, all while executing the design work myself as part of the product team.

02 / Systems-minded

Builds the shared language, not just the screen.

Turns scattered UI decisions into a system everyone can point to, and defends it after launch.

Practical example: At Strata Decision Technology, I identified and led a brand refresh and design-system rollout for a SaaS platform used by major healthcare systems, backed by qualitative and quantitative user research, so every team was building from the same source of truth.

03 / Plainspoken teacher

Makes the process legible to the whole room.

Years spent turning product design into curriculum means the explanation is never the weak link.

Practical example: Years as a Senior Product Design Instructor at Flatiron School, plus a custom design-training program built for active-duty soldiers at the U.S. Army Software Factory, taught me how to make complex product thinking land for a room that's never seen a wireframe.

04 / Accessible by default

Treats WCAG as a baseline, not a checkbox.

Accessibility gets built in at the wireframe, not patched on before ship.

Practical example: Every curriculum and every student project I oversaw at Flatiron had to meet WCAG accessibility standards, not as a final review step, but as part of how the work got built from day one.
